# Anshul Voice Layer — Chapter Writing

This is the voice authority for chapter drafting. It sits **above** `writing-style.md`
on questions of voice, thought movement, humour, uncertainty, and AI-fingerprint
removal. It sits **below** `writing-style.md` on book house formatting.

The full precedence order lives in SKILL.md. Read it before resolving any conflict.

---

## The core rule

**Reconstruct the path of the thought. Do not summarise the thought into a polished
thesis and then write backwards from it.**

Anshul discovers the position while explaining it. A chapter that states its
conclusion and then decorates it with evidence is not in his voice, even if every
sentence is simple and every analogy lands. The reader should be able to feel how
the argument was arrived at.

This is the single most common failure mode. Everything below is downstream of it.

---

## 1. First-person policy

Chapters are authorial nonfiction prose by default. Explanation, mechanism,
evidence, and the Tech/PM lens are written without "I".

"I" is permitted, and often required, at the **seams**:

- **Chapter and section openings** — where the thought started. Something Anshul
  saw, tried, ran into, or stopped believing.
- **Turns** — the moment the argument changes direction, concedes something, or
  revises an earlier claim in the same chapter.
- **Position statements** — where the chapter commits to a view that is Anshul's
  rather than the evidence's.
- **The honest edge** — the closing acknowledgement of what remains unresolved.
- **Decisions and cuts** — "I stopped doing X because", "I was wrong about this
  for two years".

Use "I" to expose reasoning, never to perform vulnerability or manufacture intimacy.
An ordinary uncertainty is not a personal journey.

Rough target: a 5,000-word chapter carries first person in roughly the opening beat,
two or three turns, and the close. If "I" is appearing in the middle of a mechanism
explanation, it is probably in the wrong place.

---

## 2. Where uncertainty goes

The chapter takes a position. The reader transformation must land cleanly and the
investment thesis must be stateable in one sentence. That is not negotiable.

Genuine uncertainty is preserved, but it is **placed**, not sprinkled:

- at section closes, where a resolved point opens a new problem,
- at the chapter close, as the honest remaining edge,
- at any point where the evidence package itself is thin or the blueprint marks a
  gap as unresolved.

Permitted, when the underlying thought is genuinely provisional:
"I am still not sure", "I think this is where", "maybe the better way is",
"I may see this differently in a year".

**Not permitted anywhere:** vague academic hedging. "It could be argued that",
"some might suggest", "in a sense", "arguably". That is not uncertainty, it is
filler, and both style layers ban it.

The test: does the qualification reflect a real limit of what is known, or is it
protecting the writer from committing? Keep the first, cut the second.

Do not let polish make the ending more certain than the underlying thinking.

---

## 3. Self-correction is structural, not decorative

A recurring and authentic movement is:

**initial instinct → friction or counterpoint → concrete example or constraint → revised position**

Use it where the underlying thought actually moved. Preserve the counterargument
that made it move; do not smooth it away in editing.

Do **not** mechanically sprinkle "but", "actually", "maybe", "on the other hand"
into paragraphs to simulate thinking. The trait being reproduced is the willingness
to revise a position, not the vocabulary of revision.

---

## 4. Humour

Humour is deliberate and load-bearing. It exists to keep the reader with you
through dense material, not to decorate.

The forms that work:

- **Analogies that make you smile** — the comparison is doing real explanatory
  work and is also funny.
- **Absurdist extension of a real idea** — follow the logic one step past where
  it stays comfortable.
- **Self-aware observation** — name the strangeness of an idea before the reader
  does. This builds trust.
- **A punchy close** — one line at the end of a section that lands the idea better
  than two more paragraphs would.
- **Dry understatement** — the safest form. When in doubt, use this.

Rules:

- Never forced, never at the reader's expense, never at the expense of a named
  company or person in the evidence package.
- **Preserve intentional humour; never manufacture it.** If a passage is not funny
  and does not want to be, leave it. Bolting jokes onto an argument reads worse
  than a dry chapter.
- Humour never replaces the explanation. If the joke is the only thing in the
  paragraph, the paragraph is not finished.
- Functional heading icons are permitted by the integrated author-approved contract. Use them sparingly when the author reference warrants them; never decorate every paragraph or evidence attribution.

## 5. Rhythm inside the book's constraints

The book requires paragraphs of 3 to 7 sentences and bans stacked one-line
sentences. Anshul's uneven rhythm operates **inside** that envelope, not against it.

Within a paragraph: mix short direct sentences, medium explanatory ones, and the
occasional longer sentence carrying several connected thoughts. Perfectly
symmetrical paragraphs are a fingerprint. A fragment is acceptable where it sounds
natural.

The short sentence after a long passage is punctuation, not decoration. It names
the point. Never stack two short sentences unless the second is a deliberate
contrast or escalation.

Connective tissue stays plain: but, because, so, maybe, actually, hence, on the
other hand. Do not upgrade simple reasoning into ornate transitions.

Contractions are used naturally. "Human" does not mean intentionally incorrect:
spelling, grammar and capitalisation are clean. Anshul's chat shorthand is never
reproduced in manuscript prose.

---

## 6. Concrete over abstract, always

The voice becomes most natural when an abstract idea is tied to a real constraint,
example, failure, trade-off, or decision.

Prefer: what was tried, what did not work, why it did not work, what changed in
the decision, what practical consequence follows.

If an example can make the idea concrete, use the example rather than another
paragraph of explanation. Generic examples such as "imagine a company that uses AI
to improve customer experience" are worse than no example.

## 7. AI-fingerprint pass

Run this after the chapter is written, as a final pass. Two checks only.

### Structure — does it *look* generated?

Watch for: uniform paragraph shapes, mechanically regular formatting, listification
(bullets because bullets are easy), too many headings or bold labels, repeated
rhetorical templates such as "not X, but Y", repeated groups of three, repeated
rhetorical questions, formulaic openings, generic summary conclusions that neatly
restate everything, superficial trailing clauses such as "highlighting…",
"underscoring…", "showcasing…".

### Language — does it *sound* generated?

Watchlist vocabulary, treated as suspects and not as banned words: delve, pivotal,
tapestry, realm, landscape, underscore, testament, foster, embark, meticulous,
vibrant, showcase, robust, seamless, leverage, transformative, multifaceted,
navigate, harness, elevate, empower, streamline, bolster, facilitate, nuanced,
ecosystem, myriad, plethora, holistic, synergy, paradigm, cornerstone, paramount,
scalable, compelling, innovative.

Watchlist phrases: "In today's rapidly evolving…", "It is important to note…",
"It is worth noting…", "Moreover…", "Furthermore…", "Additionally…", "At its core…",
"serves as a testament to…", "plays a crucial role…", "marking a pivotal moment…",
"Let's explore…", "Whether you're X or Y…", "Studies show…" without a source.

### The two rules that govern this pass

**Clustering is the signal.** One tell means little. Several tells in one passage
is the signal. A single "robust" in a sentence about infrastructure is fine. Keep
any watchlist word that is the most precise word, is standard terminology in the
domain, or would cost clarity to replace.

**Minimum intervention.** Humanise by subtraction, not invention. Patch only the
spans that are genuinely AI-coded. Prefer a local edit over a sentence rewrite, a
sentence rewrite over a paragraph rewrite, a paragraph rewrite over a full rewrite.
Never invent anecdotes, numbers, examples, or opinions to make prose feel human,
and never introduce errors deliberately.

Do not apply this pass to Punch Lines, which are preserved verbatim, or to direct
quotations from the evidence package.

## 8. Feedback routing

When Anshul reacts to a draft, diagnose the layer before rewriting:

- **"Too polished"** → remove smoothing and rhetorical completion. Do not degrade
  grammar.
- **"Machine generated"** → break predictable structure, cut AI connective tissue,
  restore the thought's actual evolution.
- **"Mechanical"** → stop enumerating everything. Focus on the few ideas with real
  consequence.
- **"Make it concise"** → cut repetition, setup, and low-value explanation. Keep
  the reasoning.
- **"More impactful"** → strengthen the concrete tension or the example. Never add
  hype.
- **"Doesn't sound like me"** → check thought development before touching
  vocabulary. It is almost always the reasoning path, not the word choice.
- **"This doesn't flow"** → structural problem, not a voice problem. Check causality
  between sections against the blueprint before editing prose.
- **"The ending isn't right"** → the close probably became more certain or neater
  than the real position. Restore the honest edge.

Preserve good work from unaffected layers. Do not regenerate a chapter for a local
problem.

---

## 9. Voice quality gate

Before delivering, answer internally:

- Does this read like someone thinking, or someone presenting a finished framework?
- If the underlying thought contained a change of mind, trade-off, or real
  uncertainty, did it survive?
- Did I add any lesson, claim, or conclusion that was not in the source material?
- Are the transitions too clean? Are the paragraphs too symmetrical?
- Is the ending suspiciously quotable?
- Is the first person doing work, or performing?
- Could I cut 10 to 20 percent of generic setup without losing the thought?

If the answers reveal machine polish, revise once before delivering.

---

## Governing principle

**Do not optimise every paragraph independently. Optimise the reader's journey
through the idea.**

The finished chapter should preserve: Anshul's thinking, the intellectual rigour,
the narrative progression, his voice, the book's formatting, and the evidence
discipline.
