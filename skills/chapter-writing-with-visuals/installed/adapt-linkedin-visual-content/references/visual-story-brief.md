# Visual Story Brief contract

Pass the exact on-image copy with this brief. It defines meaning and composition logic, not styling.

## Communication mode

Declare **text-led** or **diagram-led** and justify it from the source. Default to text-led. Use diagram-led only when a spatial relationship is necessary for understanding.

## Comprehension chain

Record the exact copy that explains what the idea is, how or why it works, and why it matters. Use connected sentences; labels do not replace explanation.

## Semantic spine

Name the single relationship that carries the argument: sequence, loop, matrix, contrast, layered system, orbit, spectrum, funnel, gate, hierarchy, flywheel, or a source-specific form.

Complete this sentence:

`One [mechanism or scene] carries [the claim], [the progression or relationship], and [the conclusion] as the reader moves [reading path].`

If the sentence needs separate mechanisms joined by “and,” split the post or rethink the spine.

## Visual embodiment

Describe how the semantic spine becomes one continuous scene, diagram, or typographic field. In text-led mode, let the copy occupy the field and use imagery only where it clarifies the argument. In diagram-led mode, place explanation on or within the relationship rather than in detached cards around it.

## Binding map

Map every meaning-bearing element—hook, explanation, stages or axes, consequence, conclusion, and signature—to the same visual field. State how each element is anchored to the spine. Copy roles define hierarchy; they must not become a stack of independent containers.

## Reading path

Describe one continuous eye path through the semantic spine. Avoid a path that merely lists separate zones such as header → cards → takeaway → footer.

## Content constraints

- **Mandatory:** exact labels, stages, numbers, caveats, relationships, and verbatim terms.
- **Flexible:** details that may move, shorten, or disappear without changing meaning.
- **Prohibited decomposition:** the specific modular fallback to avoid for this topic, such as four detached stage cards around a decorative loop.

## Preflight tests

Reject the brief before rendering when any answer is yes:

1. Can the proposed headline, diagram, explanation, or conclusion be independently cropped or replaced while the rest still works?
2. Does the plan rely on a central illustration plus surrounding cards instead of one embodied mechanism?
3. Would an unrelated title still fit the proposed architecture?
4. Is a separate takeaway box being used only because “takeaway” was named as a copy role?
