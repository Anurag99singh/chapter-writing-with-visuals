# Gold-standard quality bar

The three images in `assets/gold-standards/` are the minimum visual benchmark. They are quality references, not reusable layouts.

## What makes them work

### Visual Notebook — Program, don't prompt

- The hook dominates the first read.
- The five-step workflow visually explains the idea.
- Hand-drawn icons, arrows, emphasis marks, and annotation feel authored rather than mechanically arranged.
- The takeaway closes the argument instead of repeating the hook.

### Neon Intelligence — Human control

- The 2×2 matrix is the idea, not decoration.
- Axes and quadrants make the decision logic understandable at a glance.
- Glow is selective and supports the technical diagram.
- The composition feels complete without becoming crowded.

### Editorial Impact — The operating layer

- A concrete engine metaphor creates a strong focal point.
- Supporting controls orbit the engine and explain the system relationship.
- Typography has editorial confidence and meaningful scale contrast.
- The bottom line gives the reader a memorable conclusion.

## Mandatory checks

Reject and redesign when any answer is “no”:

1. Can the reader identify the hook immediately on a phone?
2. Can the reader understand what the idea is, how or why it works, and why it matters without the caption?
3. Does one semantic field carry the claim, progression or relationship, and conclusion?
4. Does the post retain the source's mechanism or decision logic—not only its conclusion?
5. Is the composition specifically about this topic?
6. Is the explanation written in simple, connected language rather than reduced to labels? Short paragraphs are allowed when they improve understanding.
7. Is the signature visible, photographic, accurate, and subordinate?
8. Would removing or replacing a major region break the visual argument rather than leave a generic poster behind?
9. Is the graphic subordinate to comprehension, using only the space it earns?
10. Does the portrait-ready base contain every meaning-bearing element before portrait placement? Treat this as production integrity, not evidence of visual unity.

## Text-first mobile checks

- In text-led mode, reserve approximately 55–70% of usable canvas for text communication and approximately 25–35% for supporting illustration.
- Require explanatory body copy of at least 30 px, necessary diagram labels of at least 28 px, and takeaway copy of at least 34 px on a 1080 px-wide canvas.
- Inspect at 390 px width. A reader must be able to follow the explanation without zooming.
- Reduce artwork before reducing text size or removing reasoning.
- Reject concept names without a simple explanation of what they mean or do.

## Architecture rejection test

Reject when an unrelated title still fits the architecture, or when the headline, diagram, explanation, conclusion, or signature can be independently cropped or replaced while the rest still works. A concept-specific centre surrounded by generic cards is still assembled.

## Batch comparison

Place the batch in a contact sheet. Shared branding should be obvious, but the silhouettes and focal structures should vary. If most posts have the same header, grid, takeaway box, and empty-space pattern, stop the batch and redesign before delivery.
