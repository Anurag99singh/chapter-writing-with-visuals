# News Signal and source-card formats

Use these formats for timely, source-backed LinkedIn posts. They are composition formats alongside the four core design systems and the five dense reference layouts.

## News Signal

Use when a current development is tied to a named company, product or leader and the featured person has a material role. Require a prominent identity-faithful face of a relevant leader, an accurate official company logo or wordmark when available, a high-impact source-faithful headline, readable factual subheadline and compact Anshul signature. For each leader, retrieve and inspect an actual, clearly attributed photograph from an official company profile or another reliable source. Supply that photograph as an identity reference to the whole-image generator; a name-only prompt is insufficient. Preserve distinctive eyewear, face proportions, hairstyle and age, and compare the rendered face directly with the reference before delivery. If the likeness differs, regenerate the complete poster; do not approve a generic lookalike. Prefer a widely recognized relevant founder or leader when the user asks, such as N.R. Narayana Murthy for Infosys/Indian IT sector imagery, while avoiding any implication of a personal quote. A laptop, robot or office alone does not fulfill the default News Signal format. Keep the news meaning dominant; the portrait and logo support recognition.

Do not use for generic frameworks, abstract concepts or a celebrity chosen only for reach. Use Sam Altman for relevant OpenAI stories and Dario Amodei for relevant Anthropic stories after verifying roles. For sector news, choose a leader from a company explicitly covered, keep the claim sector-wide and do not imply the person supplied a quote or owns the entire story. Keep the image focused on reported facts. Do not include MY TAKE or an opinion paragraph on the image; the accompanying caption may carry interpretation and speculation, clearly distinguished from reported fact. Never invent a logo or deliver a distorted mark. Use a verified company mark as identity guidance and visually inspect it; fall back to a plain company label when accuracy cannot be verified. Never simulate engagement metrics or let the portrait push the claim below the fold.

## Source Card

Use when the original X/Twitter post, executive statement or source artifact is itself the news. Preserve the source card as the central evidence. Add a restrained headline or context line only when it helps the reader understand why the source matters. Keep the original author, wording and source identity intact; do not recreate a tweet from memory.

Reference image:

- `assets/news-signal/news-signal-source-card.jpg` — source card takes the central place with a smaller contextual treatment.

For a real post, verify the source URL and date separately. Put the source link in the LinkedIn caption and label interpretation as interpretation.

## Author Insight Card

Use for Anshul's own concise observation, question or mental model. Use a clean white or warm surface, large readable text, the approved portrait/name and no fake platform metadata or engagement counts. Keep the image short; let the caption carry the argument.
