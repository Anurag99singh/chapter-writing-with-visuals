# Dense reference layouts for LinkedIn

## Intent and selection

Anshul supplied five screenshots and explicitly wants inspiration from their visual style and the amount of useful information packed into a single post. The saved assets are third-party references, not output templates to paste or recolour. Preserve the visual grammar; write original topic-specific content. Do not transfer another creator's identity, engagement counts, watermarks, claims, or LinkedIn interface.

Use this mode when those references or dense saveable posts are requested. It is an option alongside the existing styles, not a universal replacement. A screenshot's palette alone is insufficient: carry over columns, rows, information layers, text-to-graphic ratio, typography and annotation style.

## Shared density rules

- Build two reading levels: a strong headline/subtitle at feed size, then structured details worth opening and zooming into.
- Aim for roughly 180–350 useful words on a portrait post, adjusting to the reference and subject. These are practical targets, not quotas. Count useful facts, steps, questions or examples rather than filler.
- Use a compact header, narrow gutters and restrained icons. Devote approximately 75–85% of the area to substantive content. Avoid giant icons, broad empty margins and oversized signature regions.
- Preserve hierarchy: headline, category/stage title, concise explanation, then example or action. Keep body text crisp at full size; detail may require opening the image. Do not claim every detail is legible at 390 px width.
- Prefer a high-resolution 4:5 portrait render. At 1080 px equivalent, headline about 48–64 px, category headings 25–34 px, body about 20–26 px are useful starting points. Inspect the output rather than claiming measured sizes.
- Grid cells, catalogues, comparison regions and supporting explanation panels are intentional in this mode. Judge their organization and usefulness, not the sparse-mode prohibition on modular layouts.
- A useful dense post should lose substance if a section is removed. Do not inflate density by repeating the headline, using decorative labels or inventing statistics.
- Preserve whole-image generation. Inspect the relevant screenshot before providing it as a reference. Generate a new composition; omit phone and LinkedIn UI. Keep authorship subordinate and do not imitate the reference creator.

## 1. Reference Atlas

**Asset:** `assets/references/01-reference-atlas-post.jpg`

**Best for:** interview preparation, technical glossaries, decision checklists and learning maps.

**Structure:** full-width typographic headline over three narrow columns; two or three topical sections per column; vivid blue horizontal category bars with angled ends; thin column dividers; tight bullet lists; bold the crucial term within each short line. Mostly white, black and blue. Tiny italic contextual subtitles are optional.

**Content shape:** 6–9 categories, typically 3–6 useful items in each. About 240–350 words. Group by the reader's mental model. A footer source line is enough; no large central illustration.

**Reject:** a six-card overview with giant icons; a central hub taking space from the reference material; unverified company interview attribution.

## 2. Tool-to-Role Catalogue

**Asset:** `assets/references/02-tool-role-catalogue-post.jpg`

**Best for:** tool ecosystems, specialist skills, agent stacks and role-based workflow collections.

**Structure:** dark navy grid-paper surface; compact headline with a warm brand accent; 6–10 horizontal lanes. Each lane maps a compact role/icon at one edge, multiple small tool or capability entries in the middle, and a clearly labeled professional output at the other edge. The table-like repetition is the organizing structure. Use fine borders, restrained cyan highlights and small recognizable icons where relevant.

**Content shape:** name + concrete job + input/output or caveat. About 180–300 words. When using one product's plugins, identify plugin versus task versus output accurately. Do not invent products to fill cells or claim every role is fully automated. Include price, stars or metrics only if freshly verified.

**Reject:** six huge glowing boxes with one phrase each; heavy neon fog; invented logos or inflated replacement claims.

## 3. Workflow + Artifact Map

**Asset:** `assets/references/03-workflow-artifact-map-post.jpg`

**Best for:** changes in development, operating models, governance and multi-stage AI workflows.

**Structure:** navy headline and warm subtitle; two side-by-side process diagrams on pale blue and pale green grounds in the upper half; a full-width artifact or evidence chain below; a compact 3-by-2 set of stage implications at the bottom. Precise small arrows and thin outlined boxes, with explanatory copy within each region.

**Content shape:** two real process models, 4–6 linked artifacts, then 4–6 implications or controls. Roughly 200–300 words. Include loopbacks where necessary; do not imply the newer process eliminates review or all iteration. Label an editorial adaptation as such.

**Reject:** only two oversized four-step lists; missing artifact chain; generic old-bad/new-good slogans without operational detail.

## 4. Presenter Contrast Board

**Asset:** `assets/references/04-presenter-contrast-board-post.jpg`

**Best for:** policy alternatives, management tradeoffs, contrarian choices and behaviour changes.

**Structure:** a physical whiteboard or warm paper surface, bold black handwritten headline with a red underline, red left column and green right column. A central standing presenter occupies a narrow vertical strip, with text wrapping around the silhouette. Compare the two approaches across about three shared categories, with 2–3 paired statements per category. Use thin horizontal rules and colour only the critical words.

**Content shape:** a claim, a concrete alternative and matched consequences; about 150–230 words. Keep the category comparisons aligned. The figure should point, write or otherwise participate in the scene.

**Identity:** never reuse the source creator's likeness as Anshul. Use an explicitly illustrative presenter unless the user has supplied/approved the intended person's image for this role. The existing small author-signature portrait is a separate concept.

**Reject:** replacing the central presenter with an eraser or abstract icon; generic two-column checklist; unsubstantiated statistic below the headline.

## 5. Six-Panel Field Guide

**Asset:** `assets/references/05-six-panel-field-guide-post.jpg`

**Best for:** diagnostics, inherited systems, implementation playbooks and professional situations.

**Structure:** a photographed whiteboard aesthetic with a narrow frame and marker tray; two columns by three rows separated by thin straight rules; hand-lettered compact titles and coloured step numbers. Each cell is a miniature explanation, with a meaningful sketch, a definition or reason, and an actionable instruction. Use 2–3 small related annotations within a cell where they help.

**Content shape:** six steps, each carrying a question or principle + small diagram + action. About 200–300 words. Vary the miniature diagrams: table, split document, funnel, evidence link, test matrix. Use a coherent subject across them.

**Reject:** six one-line steps with huge icons; a single snake-shaped pipeline replacing the field-guide layout; decorative graphics with no explanation.

## Review against the reference

Before delivery, compare the candidate and reference side by side. Ask whether the characteristic silhouette, content density and hierarchy are recognizable without reading the topic. Check spelling, completeness, logical relations and text at full size; check only headline and section navigation at feed size. Reject sparse remakes when dense-reference mode was requested. Report actual limitations rather than asserting automatic virality or full mobile readability.
